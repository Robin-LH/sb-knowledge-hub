# What is WCAG?

The **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG)** are the international standard for web accessibility. Developed by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI), WCAG defines how to make web content more accessible to people with a wide range of disabilities.

## The Four Principles of Accessibility (POUR)

WCAG is structured around four foundational principles. For a website to be accessible, its content and interface must be:

1. **Perceivable** — Information and user interface components must be presentable to users in ways they can perceive (it cannot be invisible to all of their senses).
   * *Example:* Providing text alternatives (alt text) for non-text content like images.
2. **Operable** — User interface components and navigation must be operable (users must be able to interact with the interface).
   * *Example:* Making all functionality available from a keyboard, so users don't require a mouse.
3. **Understandable** — Information and the operation of the user interface must be understandable.
   * *Example:* Making text content readable and predictable, and helping users avoid and correct mistakes.
4. **Robust** — Content must be robust enough that it can be interpreted reliably by a wide variety of user agents, including assistive technologies (like screen readers).
   * *Example:* Using clean, semantic HTML that conforms to web specifications.

## WCAG Conformance Levels

WCAG guidelines are divided into three levels of conformance to meet different needs and situations:

* **Level A (Minimum)** — The most basic accessibility features. Without meeting Level A, some groups of users will find it impossible to access the site.
* **Level AA (Recommended/Standard)** — The standard target for most commercial and public websites. It addresses the most common and significant barriers for disabled users. **SiteBeacon primarily monitors against Level AA criteria.**
* **Level AAA (Exceptional)** — The highest level of accessibility. Achieving this level for entire websites is challenging and often not required as a blanket standard, though parts of a site can conform.

## Why WCAG Matters

Adhering to WCAG guidelines is not just about compliance; it yields multiple real-world benefits:

* **Inclusivity:** Enables individuals with visual, auditory, motor, cognitive, and learning disabilities to browse your site.
* **SEO Benefits:** Search engine crawlers navigate pages similarly to screen readers. Clean, structured HTML improves your search rankings.
* **Legal Compliance:** Many jurisdictions worldwide require public and corporate sites to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA (e.g., ADA in the US, European Accessibility Act in the EU).

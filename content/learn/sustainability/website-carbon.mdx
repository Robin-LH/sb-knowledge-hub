# What is Website Carbon?

While the internet is often viewed as clean and intangible, its physical infrastructure has a significant environmental footprint. Data centers, transmission networks, and the devices we use to access the web are all powered by electricity, much of which is still generated from fossil fuels.

If the internet were a country, it would rank as the **4th largest polluter in the world** in terms of carbon emissions, accounting for roughly 3% to 4% of global greenhouse gas emissions.

---

## How Website Carbon is Estimated

SiteBeacon uses a standardized estimation methodology inspired by the Sustainable Web Design model to compute carbon emissions per page view. The formula considers:

1. **Data Transfer (Page Weight):** The energy required to transmit the page assets (HTML, images, JS, CSS) from the server to the browser.
2. **Server Infrastructure Energy:** The energy consumed by host servers when handling requests, computing dynamic content, and database access.
3. **Network Infrastructure Energy:** Energy consumed by routers, switches, and cell towers that transport data across the globe.
4. **End-user Device Energy:** The energy consumed by laptops, tablets, or smartphones to download, parse, and render the webpage.
5. **Grid Carbon Intensity:** The carbon footprint of the electricity grids serving the host servers and the users.

An average webpage generates about **0.5g of CO₂ per page view**. Heavy pages with unoptimized images or video can easily exceed **2.0g of CO₂ per page view**.

## Why Green Hosting Matters

A significant factor in digital sustainability is the energy source of your server host. 

* **Standard Hosting:** Powered by standard electricity grids, which rely heavily on coal, gas, and other fossil fuels.
* **Green Hosting:** Powered by hosting providers that either run their data centers on 100% renewable energy or purchase Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) or carbon offsets to mitigate their energy use.

Using a green hosting provider immediately lowers the estimated carbon emissions of your website in SiteBeacon scans.

## Practical Ways to Reduce Website Carbon

Reducing carbon emissions goes hand in hand with improving performance:

* **Optimize Media Assets:** Compress images, avoid auto-playing videos, and use SVG graphics where possible instead of raster formats.
* **Streamline JavaScript:** Extra tracking pixels, ads, and bloated frameworks increase both CPU load (consuming device battery/energy) and transfer weight.
* **Leverage Caching:** Set long cache lifetimes so return visitors don't have to download the same assets repeatedly.
* **Use System Fonts:** Limit the number of custom web font files loaded.
* **Choose Green Infrastructure:** Host your website with verified green hosting providers (e.g., directory listings via the Green Web Foundation).
